What to Bring to Game Night: The Tabletop Player's Checklist

Packing for game night is a skill, and most players learn it the hard way — by arriving with a character sheet, no pencil, and a bag of cheese dust. Here's what to bring to game night so you're the prepared one at the table, not the cautionary tale.

The non-negotiables

  • Your dice. Bring your own. Borrowing the game master's set is fine once; it's not a lifestyle. Two sets plus a backup covers superstition-related substitutions mid-session — and if one betrays you publicly, that's what dice jail is for.
  • Your character sheet — plus a backup. A photo on your phone or a second printed copy. Character sheets have a supernatural talent for staying home on the kitchen counter.
  • Pencils, plural, and an eraser. Not a pen. Hit points change. Resources change. That "permanent" character decision in session two? It changes.
  • A notebook or scratch paper. For NPC names, the riddle you absolutely will not remember, and tallying how many times the rogue says "I check for traps."

The table fuel

  • Snacks, with rules. Shareable beats personal, dry beats greasy — chip dust on character sheets, cards and dice is a crime against the table. Nothing that needs cutlery. If the host says "bring nothing," bring a small shareable snack anyway; that's the move.
  • Your own drink vessel. This is the hill we die on: a dedicated game-night mug or bottle. No cup mix-ups, fewer spills near the battle map, and one less thing for the host to wash. The etiquette kit (bring this too)

  • A charged phone — if your sheet or rules app lives on it. Bring the paper backup anyway.
  • Working knowledge of your own abilities. Read your spells and features before your turn. The table forgives a bad roll; it remembers a ten-minute rules lookup forever.
  • Punctuality. The session starts when everyone arrives. Being the reason it starts forty minutes late is a debuff with no saving throw.

What the host usually covers

The space, the table, usually the battle map and the game's core books. Some groups rotate snacks; some treat the host as snack sovereign. Check the group chat before arriving with your entire dice hoard and a casserole — and if you're the host assembling supplies for the first time, our tabletop RPG gift guide doubles as a decent gear list.

The one thing everyone forgets

Extra dice for the new player. Some tables gain a first-timer who owns nothing, and the veteran who produces a spare set instantly becomes that person's favorite party member. Keep a loaner set in the bag. It costs little, and it's the highest-charisma move in the hobby.
