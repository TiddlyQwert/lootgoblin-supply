What to Bring to Game Night: The Tabletop Player's Packing List

Knowing what to bring to game night is the difference between settling in for four hours of adventure and spending round one borrowing a pencil. Whether it's your first session or your five-hundredth, here's the packing list — gear first, then the etiquette that makes hosts invite you back.

The core kit (non-negotiable)

  • Dice — two sets. Tonight's rollers plus a backup set, in a padded bag (if your storage situation needs work, see how to store dice). Bring your favorites, not your whole vault.
  • Character sheet + actual pencil. Two pencils, really — one always vanishes into the same dimension as missing socks. Digital sheets are fine if that's the table's norm; ask first.
  • An eraser that works. Hit points will change. Often. Sometimes with feeling.
  • Your rules references. Whatever book or app your table uses for your class. Looking something up on your own turn is fine; making the table wait while you find wifi is not.

The etiquette items (what actually gets you invited back)

  • Shareable snacks. The hobby's one universal rule. Check allergies and house rules, and pick sheet-safe food — pretzels and candy over grease bombs. Snacks never go where dice land.
  • Your own drink, ideally contained. A full soda can next to a battle map is a horror one-shot waiting to happen. A personal mug is the veteran move (our best mugs for game night list matches mugs to player types if you need one).
  • On-time arrival. Not gear, but it travels in the same bag. Six people rearranged their week for this; be there when initiative is rolled.
  • Phone on silent. The group chat can wait four hours. Probably.

The nice-to-haves (table veteran tier)

  • A dice tray. Keeps rolls contained, protects the host's table, and ends "that didn't count, it hit the floor" debates. Borrowing is fine; owning is better — the dice tray gift guide covers what to look for.
  • A small notebook. For NPC names, loot tallies, and the thing the game master definitely said three sessions ago.
  • A spare everything. Extra dice, extra pencil, extra sheet for the player who forgot theirs. There's one at every table. This week, it isn't you.

One rule above all

Whatever you bring, observe the hobby's oldest etiquette: ask before touching another player's dice. It's half superstition (covered in full in dice superstitions) and half simple respect — dice are personal, often gifts, and sometimes the product of serious hoarding.
