Dice Tower vs Dice Tray: Which One Does Your Table Actually Need?

At some point every tabletop group has the debate: dice tower vs dice tray. One side swears by the clatter of a tower; the other insists a quiet padded tray is all a civilized table needs. They're not actually rivals — they do different jobs — but if you're spending money on exactly one, here's the honest breakdown from the dice goblin trenches.

What each one actually does

A dice tray is a landing zone: a padded, walled area that keeps rolls on the table, off the floor, and away from the miniatures. It protects the table from metal dice and protects metal dice from the table. A dice tower is a randomizer: the die goes in the top, bounces through internal ramps, and tumbles out the front — no wrist technique involved. One contains your roll; the other makes your roll.

The case for the tower

  • True randomization. If anyone at your table has a "lucky roll technique," the tower settles the argument permanently.
  • The drama. Dropping a die into a tower for a death save feels ceremonial. Tables remember tower rolls.
  • Contained chaos. Wild throwers can't launch dice into the snacks if the dice go through a tower first.

The downsides are real: towers are loud, they eat table space, they travel badly, and some tables ban them outright — for the noise, for superstition (yes, "the tower pre-rolls the luck out" is a genuine belief; more in dice superstitions), or because a game master wants to watch dice tumble in the open.

The case for the tray

  • Quiet and compact. Felt-lined trays are nearly silent, and folding trays pack flat.
  • Travels well. A tray fits in the game bag — it's on our what to bring to game night list for exactly that reason.
  • Doubles as storage for tonight's active set between rolls (more in how to store dice).
  • Nobody bans a tray. It's the Switzerland of table accessories.

What a tray doesn't do is randomize: your throw is still your throw, for better or worse.

The verdict

If you play at one home table and your group loves spectacle, the tower earns its footprint. If you travel to games, share table space, or play anywhere noise matters, the tray wins easily. Most tables land on the combo move: tower aimed into tray — randomization plus a guaranteed catch.
