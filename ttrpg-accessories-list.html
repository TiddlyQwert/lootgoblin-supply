TTRPG Accessories List: The Gear That Actually Gets Used

Search for tabletop accessories and you'll drown in gadget lists. Most of it gathers dust. This is the shorter, honest version: the accessories players actually reach for every session, in the order they earn their place in the bag. It covers your personal kit — for the table's shared gear, see game night essentials; for the packing version, what to bring to game night.

The big three: tray, bag, tower

  • Dice tray. The single most-used accessory in the hobby. Ends "that roll hit the floor, it doesn't count" debates, protects the table from metal dice, and travels flat. The dice tray gift guide covers what separates a good one from a flimsy one.
  • Dice bag. The hoard's home between sessions. Size it for the collection you'll have next year, not the one you have now — goblin math is real. Picking criteria live in the dice bag gift guide.
  • Dice tower. Optional, theatrical, beloved. A tower randomizes rolls and makes big moments louder — but it's an upgrade, not a need. The full case for and against: dice tower vs dice tray.

Drinkware: the underrated accessory

A dedicated table mug is safety equipment. Open cans tip; a heavy mug with a handle survives elbow bumps, and having your vessel means nobody's cup migrates into the dice zone. The Game Master — I Make It Up As I Go mug ($16.99) marks the seat behind the screen, and we matched all eight mugs to table personalities in best mugs for game night.

The off-table tier: wear the hobby

  • A hat. Session-zero introductions, convention days, sun at the outdoor one-shot. The Dice Goblin Dad Hat ($27.99) declares the hoard without saying a word.
  • Stickers. The cheapest accessory that exists: character folios, laptops, water bottles, notebook covers. A Probably a Mimic sticker (from $3.99) marks anything that might bite.
  • A tee for game day. Optional, but tables with a dress code have more fun — that's just science.

The paper tier

A session notebook that lives in your bag (loot tallies, NPC names, plot threads) beats six players half-remembering. Add spare pencils — plural — and a spare set of dice for the friend who forgot theirs. Dice goblins call that recruitment inventory. For organizing the rest of the collection, how to store dice covers the go-bag to vault tiers.

What to skip

Gimmicky single-use gadgets. Oversized novelty dice for actual play. And anything carrying official game branding from unofficial sellers — unlicensed merch is a rights problem and usually badly made. Buy the tray first.
