What Does "Nat 20" Mean?

A nat 20 — short for natural 20 — is when a twenty-sided die (d20) lands with the 20 face up, before any modifiers are added or subtracted. It's the highest possible raw result on the die, and in most tabletop RPGs it means the action succeeds as well as it possibly can: a critical hit, a spectacular save, a persuasion so smooth the guard thanks you for robbing him.

"Natural" vs. modified

The distinction matters at the table. If you roll a 14 and your +6 bonus makes it 20, that's a modified 20 — good, but mundane. A natural 20 is the die itself showing 20. Many game systems and house rules give nat 20s special treatment: automatic hits, double damage, or pure table-celebration energy. The opposite is the nat 1 — the "critical fail" — which produces the opposite sound from the table.

Why it became a culture, not just a rule

Rolling a nat 20 is a 5% chance that feels like destiny. Tables erupt. Players retell their nat-20 moments for years ("so then, against the dragon, I rolled..."). Actual-play shows put slow-motion replays on them. Somewhere along the way, "nat 20" stopped being dice terminology and became shorthand for any improbably perfect outcome — on charisma checks and job interviews alike.

Nat 20 etiquette

  • You are allowed one (1) reenactment of the roll per session.
  • The d20 that rolled it is now "the lucky one." This is not negotiable.
  • If it happens at a dramatic story moment, the Game Master is legally required to describe something cool.
