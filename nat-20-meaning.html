What Does “Nat 20” Mean?

A nat 20 — short for natural 20 — is when a twenty-sided die (d20) physically lands with the 20 face up, before any modifiers are added. In Dungeons & Dragons and most tabletop RPGs built on the d20, it is the best possible roll: on an attack roll, a natural 20 is an automatic hit and a critical hit, which means extra damage dice.

Outside the rules, “nat 20” has become tabletop shorthand for a perfect outcome. Rolling one at a dramatic moment is the dice goblin's holy grail — the table erupts, the GM sighs, and the story gets a new chapter.

Nat 20 vs. a “dirty 20”

Not all twenties are equal. A natural 20 is the die showing 20. A dirty 20 is reaching a total of 20 after adding modifiers — say, a 14 on the die plus a +6 bonus. A dirty 20 is still a great roll and will usually succeed, but it doesn't trigger the automatic-hit and critical-hit rules of a natural 20.

Does a nat 20 always succeed?

By the book: on attack rolls, yes — a natural 20 always hits and always crits. On ability checks and saving throws, the official rules don't grant automatic success… but “nat 20 auto-succeeds” is one of the most popular house rules in the hobby, and plenty of tables play it that way. (Ask your GM before you try to seduce the dragon.)

The odds, and the other end of the die

A fair d20 shows a 20 exactly 1 time in 20 — a flat 5% chance per roll, the same as any other face. The opposite of the nat 20 is the nat 1: the critical fumble, the automatic miss, the moment the rogue trips over their own cape. Veteran players treasure both outcomes — the nat 1 makes the nat 20 taste sweeter.

Why “nat 20” escaped the table

Actual-play shows and RPG TikTok turned the term into general internet slang for a wildly lucky outcome: “walked into the interview and rolled a nat 20.” It now lives on shirts, mugs and tattoos — which is exactly where we come in.
