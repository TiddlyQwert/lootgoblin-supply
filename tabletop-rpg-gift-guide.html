Tabletop RPG Gift Guide: What to Actually Get a D&D Player

Buying for a tabletop RPG player is easy once you accept one truth: they do not need anything, and they will treasure everything. Below is the field guide — sorted by who they are at the table, with rough budgets, so you don't panic-buy a d20-shaped candle.

For the dice goblin (the collector)

  1. One really good set of dice. Not a bulk bag — one sharp-edged, hand-picked set in their favorite color. Budget: $10–30.
  2. A dice tray. The ritual object every goblin deserves. See our full dice tray gift guide. Budget: $15–50.
  3. A dice jail. For the d20 that rolled three nat 1s in a row. It is a real product and it is always funny. Budget: $10–15.
  4. The uniform. The Dice Goblin T-Shirt or the smug-dragon "My Other Hoard Is Dice" mug. Budget: $17–24.

For the Game Master (the overworked one)

  1. A nice notebook. Dotted or dot-grid. Their prep notes will outlive the campaign. Budget: $10–25.
  2. Index cards + a decent pen. The unglamorous GM superpower. Budget: under $10.
  3. A GM screen they'll actually use. Check what system they run first — screens are system-specific. Budget: $15–40.
  4. The confession. The "Game Master — I Make It Up As I Go" shirt (also a mug). More in our game master gift ideas. Budget: $17–24.

For the chaos player (the table gremlin)

  1. The "Crit Happens" tee. For the one whose failures are campaign legend. Budget: ~$24.
  2. The "Nat 20 Energy" shirt. For the one who peaked with a single roll in 2019. Budget: ~$24.
  3. A spare set of "loaner dice." So they stop borrowing yours. Budget: $8–12.

For the brand-new player

  1. A starter dice set + a simple tray. Everything they need to show up to session one looking like they belong. Budget: $20–35.
  2. The "Powered by Dice & Questionable Decisions" tee. Accurate from day one. Budget: ~$24.

Universal truths of RPG gifting

  • When in doubt, dice. When still in doubt, shinier dice.
  • Never buy someone a "better" version of the thing they clearly love — buy them the second one.
  • A gift that references their character or their table stories beats an expensive generic one every time.
