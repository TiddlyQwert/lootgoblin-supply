Tabletop RPG Stocking Stuffers: Small Gifts That Land a Crit

Stockings are a trap. The size limit pushes you toward novelty junk that gets quietly thrown out by New Year's. But tabletop players are the glorious exception, because the hobby runs on small, affordable, genuinely useful objects. You can fill a stocking for a D&D player or game master with zero filler — here's the formula.

1. The golden rule: consumable or personal

A good stocking stuffer either gets used up (so it never becomes clutter) or is so specific to the person that it can't feel generic. Dice somehow hit both: every player uses them constantly, and no player has ever had "enough." When in doubt, dice.

2. The under-$10 wins

  • One really nice set of polyhedral dice. Find out their favorite color first — a set in their color beats a random pretty set every time.
  • A spare d20. The die that rolls badly gets blamed and benched; backups are always welcome. A jumbo d20 works as the joke-that's-actually-useful.
  • A pocket notebook and a decent pencil. For character notes, NPC names they'll otherwise forget, and tracking whose turn it is to bring snacks.
  • Game-night fuel. A good bag of coffee or tea — the hobby runs on it.

3. The mug strat: the anchor every stocking needs

A mug is the biggest item that still fits in a stocking — tip it in sideways and the whole thing immediately feels substantial instead of sad. The trick is picking one that says who they are at the table:

  • Rolls nothing but 20s (or claims to): the Nat 20 Energy mug.
  • The party's chaos agent: the Crit Happens mug — everyone at the table has a crit-fail story about them.
  • The forever-GM: the Game Master — I Make It Up As I Go mug. They will laugh, then look tired.

One honest heads-up: our mugs are printed to order, which takes 2–7 business days before shipping plus carrier transit — so order by early-to-mid December for holiday delivery. Dice and notebooks from anywhere will wait; made-to-order stuff won't.

4. What to avoid

  • Anything that requires knowing their character. You will get the class, spelling, or edition wrong, and they will have to smile about it.
  • Novelty joke dice that don't roll fair. Funny once, landfill by February. Buy dice that actually roll.
  • A miniature of "their character" unless they literally sent you a link. There are thousands of minis and the wrong one is just a tiny stranger.
  • Anything that doesn't fit. A battle mat sticking out of the top of a stocking is just a regular gift with extra steps.

5. The formula

One anchor (the mug), one consumable (a set of dice), one personal small thing (the notebook, the coffee), plus the traditional candy. That's a stocking that lands a crit — and every piece of it survives past January.
