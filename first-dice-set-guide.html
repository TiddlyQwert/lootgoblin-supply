First Dice Set Guide: How to Pick Your First Set of RPG Dice

Every dice goblin started with one set — usually a cheap translucent one bought the week before a first session. This guide gets you a better first set than most of us had, without overthinking it. (Fair warning: "first set" is a temporary condition. See what is a dice goblin for your likely future.)

What's in a set

A standard tabletop set is seven polyhedral dice: d4, d6, d8, d10, d10 percentile, d12 and d20. The d20 does the heavy lifting — attacks, checks, saves — while the rest mostly roll damage and odds. One full set covers almost every system you'll sit down to; you don't need multiples until a spell or ability calls for a fistful of one type.

How much to spend

  • Under $8: gamble territory — fine in a pinch, but expect faint paint and the occasional chipped corner.
  • $8–15: the sweet spot. Solid resin or acrylic, sharp numbers, endless colors. Your first set lives here.
  • $15–40: premium resin, better swirls and inclusions. Great once you know your taste.
  • $40+: gemstone, glass and precision metal. Beautiful, collectible, and absolutely not required — that's hoard behavior, and it can wait a level or two.

The one rule that matters: readability

Buy dice you can read from arm's length in dim light. High-contrast numbers — light on dark or dark on light — beat the prettiest swirl every time. Your table will read your rolls over your shoulder, and squinting kills the drama of a nat 20. After readability: check for bubbles or voids in the material, and make sure all seven dice are actually in the bag.

Material cheat sheet

  • Resin/acrylic: light, cheap, infinite styles — the correct first choice.
  • Metal: heavy and dramatic, but loud, table-denting, and best paired with a tray (dice tower vs tray).
  • Gemstone/glass: gorgeous, chip-prone — a later indulgence, not a starter.

Beginner mistakes to skip

Don't buy a single giant d20 as your only dice. Don't start with metal before you own a rolling surface. Don't panic-buy five sets "for options" — meet your character first, then match dice to them (that's half the fun). And once you have the set, keep it safe: our how to store dice guide covers bags, boxes and the vault-tier options. New dice also attract table folklore — warm-up rolls, lucky sets, the dice jail — all documented in dice superstitions.
