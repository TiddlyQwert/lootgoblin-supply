What Is a Loot Goblin?

A loot goblin is a tabletop RPG player whose character treats every dungeon like a yard sale. Corpses? Searched. Chests? Opened before the trap check. The quest-giver's personal cutlery? Already in the bag of holding. If it isn't nailed down, it's loot. If it's nailed down, the loot goblin has a crowbar.

Like its cousin the dice goblin, the term is affectionate. The loot goblin isn't greedy — they're thorough. Every copper piece is a resource. Every mysterious potion is a future problem solved. Every tavern is a market.

Where the term comes from

"Loot goblin" grew out of video-game RPG culture (where enemies literally burst into piles of shiny loot) and crossed naturally into tabletop play, where the instinct is older than the hobby itself. Fantasy goblins hoard trinkets; players hoard everything else. Somewhere around the thousandth "I search the room," the table started calling it what it is.

Signs there's a loot goblin at your table

  • Their inventory list has an appendix.
  • They remember an item they picked up eleven sessions ago that is suddenly, crucially relevant.
  • The Game Master has said "you cannot loot that" to them. More than once. This week.
  • They know the resale value of a goblin's boots.
  • Encumbrance rules are a personal challenge.

In defense of loot goblins

Every party needs one. Loot goblins fund the healing potions, remember the plot items, and turn "we're broke" into "we have 37 assorted pelts and a plan." They're not hoarding — they're preparing.
