What Is a Dice Goblin?

A dice goblin is a tabletop role-playing game player who collects dice — far, far beyond what any game could ever require. One set of seven polyhedral dice is enough to play almost any tabletop RPG. A dice goblin owns thirty sets. And a dice tray. And a dice bag shaped like a mimic. And a separate bag for the "good" dice.

The term is affectionate, not insulting. Dice goblins are the backbone of every game table: they always have spare dice for new players, they know exactly which d20 is "lucky," and their eyes light up when someone mentions metal dice, gemstone dice, or a new Kickstarter.

Where did the term come from?

"Goblin" in gaming slang describes someone who hoards shiny things — borrowed from fantasy goblins and their loot piles. As tabletop RPGs exploded in popularity (the hobby passed 50 million players), dice collecting became its own sub-hobby: sharp-edged resin sets, liquid-core dice, dice that light up. The community needed a word for the people buying them all. "Dice goblin" stuck, and it spread through Reddit, TikTok and actual-play shows.

Signs you might be a dice goblin

  • You own more dice sets than there are players at your table — and you can name most of them.
  • You have a "jail" for dice that roll badly.
  • You've bought dice for a character concept you haven't played yet.
  • You say "just one more set" the way dragons say "just one more hoard."
  • Someone at your table asks to borrow a d4 and you ask "which color?"

Dice goblin vs. loot goblin

Close relatives. A loot goblin hoards everything — dice, minis, rulebooks, snacks, possibly the GM's notes. A dice goblin's obsession is specifically polyhedral. Most of us are both.
