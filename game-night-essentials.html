Game Night Essentials: The Gear That Upgrades Every Session

Every table has a kit — the stuff that lives at the game and makes sessions run smoothly. It's different from a personal packing list (that's what to bring to game night); these are the game night essentials the table itself needs, ranked in the order they pay for themselves.

The core kit (every table, every week)

  • Dice — including a house set. Players bring their own, but a table stash of spare dice saves the night when someone forgets theirs or a newcomer joins. Dice goblins call this "recruitment inventory."
  • Pencils and paper. Real pencils, plural, plus scrap paper for initiative order and NPC names. The table that hunts for a pencil every round loses twenty minutes a session.
  • Rules reference within reach. Whatever book or app your game uses, kept at arm's length — not across the room under a backpack.
  • Contained drinks and sheet-safe snacks. More on both below, because they're the two biggest threats to the campaign.

Drinkware is safety equipment

The number one game-night casualty isn't a character — it's a character sheet, killed by a tipped soda. Veteran tables run three rules: every drink lives in a contained vessel with some heft (a proper mug beats an open can every time), drinks stay out of the dice-and-map zone, and coasters mark where cups are allowed. A dedicated table mug is the cheapest insurance in the hobby: the Game Master — I Make It Up As I Go mug marks the host's seat, and the Nat 20 Energy tee is the dress code for the player who keeps rolling suspiciously well. The comfort tier (worth it by session three)

  • A dice tray. Ends "that didn't count, it hit the floor" debates forever and protects the table — the dice tray gift guide covers what actually matters in one.
  • Decent lighting. Character sheets are tiny text; a bright, warm lamp over the play area quietly improves everyone's night.
  • A session notebook. One notebook that stays with the table — loot tallies, NPC names, plot threads — beats six players half-remembering.
  • Coasters and a snack station. A side table for food keeps grease off the play area; pretzels, candy and popcorn travel from bowl to mouth without touching a sheet.

What to upgrade first

In order: 1) dice tray, 2) proper drinkware, 3) a house dice stash, 4) lighting, 5) the comfort extras. A dice tower sits further down the list — wonderful, but optional; see dice tower vs dice tray for that whole debate.
