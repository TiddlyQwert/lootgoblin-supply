How to Store Dice: Bags, Trays, Cases & Hoard Management

Nobody buys one set of dice. You buy one set, then a pretty one, then one that matches your character's vibe, and eighteen months later you're researching how to store dice at 1 a.m. because the drawer won't close. Welcome. This is the system.

The three tiers of dice storage

  1. The go-bag. What travels to game night. A drawstring dice bag comfortably holds two to five sets — anything more and you're the person digging for a d4 while the whole table waits. Picking one worthy of the hoard is its own art; we cover it in the dice bag gift guide.
  2. The table kit. At the table, dice live in a tray, not a bag. A tray keeps rolls contained, protects the table, and — critically — keeps your dice separated from everyone else's. Loose dice rolling across a crowded table is how sets get mixed and deeply held superstitions get violated. Our dice tray guide breaks down what to look for.
  3. The vault. Long-term storage for the collection: display cases, foam-lined boxes, divided organizers. This is where sets wait between campaigns — visible, organized, and not grinding against each other in a drawer.

Know your materials

  • Acrylic and resin (most sets): tough and low-maintenance, but keep them out of prolonged direct sunlight — UV yellows clear resin over time.
  • Gemstone and glass: beautiful and chip-prone. Each set gets its own padded compartment, never loose with other dice.
  • Metal: heavy and hard, which means they scratch softer dice and dent wooden tables. Bag them separately. And metal dice in a glass tray is a noise complaint waiting to happen.
  • Sharp-edge resin: the crisp-edged showpieces chip if they rattle around loose in bulk. Compartmentalize them.

Organization that actually works

  • Keep sets together. Splitting a set across containers is how dice quietly go missing between sessions.
  • Run an active roster. One bag holds your current character's sets; the vault holds everything else. Rotate when the character — or the mood — changes.
  • Label the weird ones. Percentile d10s, oversized d20s, the d30 you bought ironically — future-you will thank present-you.
  • Never store dice loose in a backpack. That's not storage, that's abrasion with extra steps.
