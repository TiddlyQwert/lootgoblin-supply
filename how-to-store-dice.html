How to Store Dice: Keep the Hoard Safe, Sorted & Ready to Roll

It starts with one set. Then a second "for backup." Then a shiny one at a convention, and suddenly you own nine pounds of click-clack math rocks and nowhere to put them. How to store dice is the quiet endgame of being a dice goblin — here's how to do it without scuffed sets or a junk-drawer avalanche.

The main storage options, ranked by use case

  • Dice bags — the classic. Soft, cheap, portable, and the default answer for everyday sets. Ideal for the sets you actually roll weekly. If you're picking one as a gift, our dice bag gift guide breaks down what makes a bag worthy of the hoard.
  • Dice trays — technically for rolling, but a good tray doubles as the "active duty" home for tonight's set. It keeps the table-safe set corralled and out of the junk zone. More in the dice tray gift guide.
  • Hard cases with foam slots — the vault. Each die gets its own padded cell. This is the right answer for metal sets, sharp-edge resin, and anything you'd be genuinely upset to chip.
  • Display storage — shelves, shadow boxes, glass jars. Some of the hoard isn't for rolling; it's for admiring. Display is legitimate storage as long as it's out of direct sunlight (more on that below).

Material rules that actually matter

Metal dice live alone. They're heavy enough to scuff acrylic sets and scratch each other's plating. Padded pouch or foam-lined case, no exceptions. Sharp-edge resin (the crisp, handmade kind) chips at the points — those deserve compartments too. Everything else — your standard acrylic and resin sets — is tougher than it looks and can share a soft bag without drama.

Two environmental rules apply to the whole hoard: keep dice out of direct sunlight (UV yellows clear resin and fades inked numbers) and away from heat (a summer car dashboard can warp cheap plastic). A drawer or closed shelf beats a sunny windowsill every time.

Organizing a growing collection

Once the hoard passes a dozen sets, storage becomes a retrieval problem. The system that works: keep one active rotation — the two or three sets you actually bring to sessions — in a bag or tray by the door, and file the rest. Sorting by complete set beats sorting by die type (a bag of 40 loose d20s is a museum, not a kit). Small labeled pouches inside a bigger container give you goblin maximalism and the ability to find the green marble set before the session starts. It's the practical side of the dice goblin aesthetic: the hoard should look like a dragon's treasure, not a kitchen drawer.

Travel storage for game night

Heading to someone else's table? The one-bag rule keeps you honest: tonight's set (plus a spare d20, because superstition is real) in a padded bag, tucked into your game bag. Leave the vault at home. For the full checklist of what else goes in that bag, see what to bring to game night.
