What Does "Crit Happens" Mean?

"Crit happens" is tabletop RPG wordplay: a collision of "crit" — short for critical hit — and the old shrug-philosophy phrase "stuff happens." Put together, it's the dice goblin's motto: the dice are going to do something dramatic sooner or later, and no amount of planning changes that. Accept it. Embrace it. Put it on a shirt.

First: what's a crit?

A critical hit is an attack roll that succeeds as hard as the rules allow. In most d20-based games it triggers on a natural 20 — the die itself showing 20 before any modifiers — and usually means double damage dice, some bonus effect, or at minimum a very loud reaction from the table. The opposite is the critical fail (often a natural 1), which produces a very different, equally loud reaction.

The two directions of "crit happens"

  • The good kind: the boss fight was going badly until somebody's dice remembered their job. Double damage. Table erupts. Somebody reenacts it at every session for the next year.
  • The other kind: the Game Master rolls one against you. The phrase still applies — it's just said more quietly, with respect, while you update your hit points.

That's the whole wisdom of it: crits cut both ways, and they arrive on their own schedule. Roughly one in twenty d20 rolls is a natural 20, which means across a full night of rolling, "crit happens" stops being a joke and starts being a statistical guarantee.

Why it became a saying

Tabletop players spend a lot of emotional energy on dice. When the swingiest moment of the game is also the most random, you get superstition, lucky dice rituals — and gallows-humor slogans. "Crit happens" belongs to the same family of dice-goblin wisdom as "the dice giveth and the dice taketh away" and "never split the party." It works as celebration, as consolation, and as fair warning to the new player at the table.
