Dice Tray Gift Guide: How to Pick One They'll Actually Use

A dice tray is the rare tabletop gift that is almost impossible to get wrong: every player rolls dice, most tables ban rolling on the bare table, and nobody has ever said "I own too many dice trays." (They have said that about dice. They were lying.) Here's how to pick one that gets used every session instead of retiring to a shelf.

1. Material is the personality

  • Wood — the heirloom option. Heavy, quiet-ish, looks like it belongs in a wizard's study. Best for home tables; less fun to carry.
  • Leather / vegan leather (snap corners) — the travel option. Lays flat in a bag, snaps into shape at the table. The right call for anyone who plays at a game store, a convention, or a friend's house.
  • Silicone / rubber — the tank. Silent rolls, survives being stepped on, cheap enough to buy two. Zero ceremony, maximum function.
  • Felt-lined anything — the quiet upgrade. Felt bottoms turn a dice avalanche into a dignified thud. Whatever material you pick, felt lining is the feature that matters most.

2. Size: bigger than you think

A tray that's too small turns every roll into a gentle placement, which defeats the entire point of rolling dice. Look for a rolling area around 8 inches across or more — enough room for a full fistful-of-d6s fireball. Bonus features that genuinely get used: a side compartment for the "good dice," and a lid if they travel with it.

3. Match the tray to the player

  • The dice goblin (owns 40+ sets, all named): go wood or go engraved. They will photograph it.
  • The Game Master: a snap-flat leather tray — they already carry the most gear, don't add bulk.
  • The new player: silicone or a simple felt-lined tray. Indestructible, zero intimidation.
  • The dice dragon (hoards dice, never rolls them): honestly, get them a display case instead. Or lean in with a "My Other Hoard Is Dice" mug.

4. Make it a bundle

A tray alone is practical; a tray plus one small personal thing is a gift. The classic combo is tray + dice, but everyone does that. Better: tray + something that says you know who they are at the table.
