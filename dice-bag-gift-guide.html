Dice Bag Gift Guide: How to Pick a Bag Worthy of the Hoard

A dice bag is the default tabletop gift for a reason: every player needs somewhere to keep the shinies. But most gifted dice bags fail in one of two ways — they're too small (bought by someone who assumes a player owns one set of dice; adorable) or too flimsy (a fistful of metal dice is basically a tiny mace, and it will win against thin fabric). Here's how to pick a bag that becomes part of their kit for years instead of retiring to a drawer by session three.

1. Capacity: think in sets, then double it

One "set" is seven polyhedral dice. A new player owns one to three sets. A confirmed dice goblin owns twenty or more and will sincerely describe that as "just the essentials." Dice collections only grow — nobody has ever downsized — so buy for at least twice the dice they currently own. A bag that holds around ten sets (70+ dice) is a safe floor for anyone more than a year into the hobby. One caveat: capacity claims assume acrylic dice. Metal dice are roughly twice as heavy and far less compressible, so halve the advertised number if their hoard is metal.

2. Structure: how the bag stands (or doesn't)

  • Drawstring pouch — the classic. Packs flat, cheap, and opens into a shallow dice pool they can roll straight out of in a pinch. Weaknesses: it tips over, and the cord eventually frays.
  • Standing / freestanding bag — the upgrade. Opens wide and stays upright on the table like a dice bowl, so everyone can admire the hoard. Best choice for a home table.
  • Zippered case with trays — the armory. Foam slots or divided compartments keep every set sorted and protected. Right for the player who arranges dice by color story; wrong for the one who just wants to grab and roll.

3. Material: the bag takes the beating so the table doesn't

  • Leather or waxed canvas — survives decades, develops character, and muffles the rattle. The heirloom pick.
  • Thick cotton with a soft lining — kind to metal dice and to your budget. Check for a double-stitched bottom seam; that's where dice bags go to die.
  • Whatever the exterior, the lining matters most. A lined interior is the difference between a dice bag and a metal-dice blender.

4. Match the bag to the goblin

  • The new player: a mid-size drawstring that holds five or more sets. They'll grow into it — and then into a proper goblin.
  • The dice goblin (40+ sets, all named): go big or go organized. A standing bag or a zippered case, and bonus points for anything that looks like it was looted from a dragon.
  • The Game Master: a standing bag. They already carry the most gear at the table, and they roll in the open for maximum drama.
  • The dice dragon (hoards dice, rarely rolls them): skip the bag and get a display case. Or lean in with a "My Other Hoard Is Dice" mug.

5. Make it a gift, not a supply run

A bag alone says "I know you play that dice game." A bag with one new set of dice already inside — or paired with something personal — says "I know you." The single cheapest upgrade to this gift is dropping one fresh set of dice into the bag before you wrap it.

Our pick: a standing, lined bag sized for ~10 sets, with one new set of dice inside, paired with the Dice Goblin T-Shirt (or the mug if they're the tea-at-the-table type). Gifting the GM instead?
